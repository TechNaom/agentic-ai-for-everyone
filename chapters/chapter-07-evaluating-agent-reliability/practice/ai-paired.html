Now Pair With an AI

A ninth scenario, not in the eight above: a team's agent, "ClaimBot", processes insurance-adjustment requests (the specific company is left unnamed on purpose, the same way Chapter 6's EscrowBot scenario did). Two real, layered problems were found in the same eval report. First: ClaimBot's dashboard reported "94% success rate" from a batch of 50 runs, but that number was computed from a task-success check only (did a claim outcome get produced at all) -- nobody had ever measured trajectory correctness, so a large fraction of those "successful" runs had actually skipped the required fraud-flag check entirely and gotten lucky on claims that didn't need it. Second, in a completely different part of the same report: the team's pass@5 number (98%) was presented as "the reliability rate" in a leadership slide, with the underlying pass@1 (61%) never mentioned anywhere in the deck.

Step 1: Diagnose it yourself, first

Before reading further: name the two distinct problems here using this chapter's vocabulary (one is about which of two metrics was measured at all; the other is about which of two pass@k numbers was reported) and write your own fix for each. There's no grading here, just your own reasoning to compare against Step 3.

Step 2: Ask an AI assistant

Thought process: this scenario has two layered problems (a missing metric AND a misleading choice of which number to headline), so the prompt needs to ask the AI to identify both, not just one -- a shallow prompt like "is 94% success rate good?" invites an answer that takes the reported number at face value instead of questioning what it actually measured.

Write the prompt you'd give an AI assistant:

Paste your prompt into your assistant of choice and compare its answer against the critique below.
Show a strong version of this prompt 
Here's my agent ClaimBot's eval report: [paste the scenario above]. Identify EVERY distinct measurement problem in this report, not just the first one you notice -- there are two -- and name each using this chapter's vocabulary (task success vs. trajectory correctness being conflated into one number, versus reporting a high pass@k without also disclosing pass@1). Propose a concrete, specific fix for each, including exactly what new number should be added to the dashboard and the slide deck.

Step 3: Critique what came back

What a complete diagnosis catches
  • Problem 1 (task success only, no trajectory correctness): a 94% "success rate" that only checks whether a claim outcome was produced says nothing about whether the fraud-flag check actually ran when it should have -- exactly the T4 pattern this chapter's own Section 10 demonstrated (100% task success, only 60% trajectory correctness). The fix is to compute and report both rates, separately, per task type.
  • Problem 2 (pass@5 without pass@1): headlining "98% reliable" from pass@5 while a 61% pass@1 sits unreported in the raw data is exactly the masking risk this chapter's own Section 11 named directly. The fix is a standing reporting rule: pass@1 is always shown alongside any higher-k number, never replaced by it.
  • A shallow AI answer often catches only Problem 2 (the more obviously "misleading slide" issue) and misses that Problem 1 is a structural measurement gap -- the team never built a trajectory-correctness check at all, not just chose not to report it.

Compare the AI's answer against your own Step 1 notes and the two-problem breakdown above. Did it catch both? Did it propose the right fix for each -- adding the missing metric, and fixing the reporting rule -- or did it conflate the two into one vague "be more transparent with metrics" suggestion?
