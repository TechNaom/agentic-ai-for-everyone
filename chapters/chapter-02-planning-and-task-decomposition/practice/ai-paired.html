Now Pair With an AI

A ninth scenario, not in the eight above: "EscalationBot", a support-ticket router at a fictional subscription service (left unnamed on purpose, the same way Chapter 1's ConciergeBot scenario did — a two-tool scenario doesn't need an invented company). EscalationBot handles billing-dispute tickets with three tools: verify_account(account_id), get_dispute_reason(ticket_id), and route_to_team(ticket_id, team). Every dispute ticket needs the same first two checks done regardless of order, but EscalationBot currently decides its next tool call fresh, one reasoning call at a time, for all three steps — even though nothing about this task's step order ever changes ticket to ticket. Separately: for one real ticket, EscalationBot pre-committed to route_to_team(ticket_id, "billing") as step 3 before reading step 2's get_dispute_reason result — which turned out to say "duplicate charge caused by a failed refund retry," a case that should have routed to the refunds team, not billing. All three tool calls succeeded; the ticket still went to the wrong team.

Step 1: Diagnose it yourself, first

Before reading further: name the two distinct problems here using this chapter's vocabulary (one is about which planning mode the task should use at all; the other is a specific failure class from Section 11 of the lesson) and write your own fix for each. There's no grading here, just your own reasoning to compare against Step 3.

Step 2: Ask an AI assistant

Thought process: this scenario has two layered problems (an inefficient planning mode AND a wrong-plan failure), so the prompt needs to ask the AI to identify both, not just one — a shallow prompt like "what's wrong with this agent's planning" invites a single, incomplete answer, usually just the more dramatic-sounding misrouted ticket.

Write the prompt you'd give an AI assistant:

Paste your prompt into your assistant of choice and compare its answer against the critique below.
Show a strong version of this prompt 
Here's my agent EscalationBot, which has three tools: verify_account(account_id), get_dispute_reason(ticket_id), route_to_team(ticket_id, team): [paste the scenario above]. Identify EVERY distinct planning problem in this description, not just the first one you notice -- there are two -- and name each using this chapter's vocabulary (wrong planning mode for the task's shape, or a wrong-plan failure). Propose a concrete, specific fix for each.

Step 3: Critique what came back

What a complete diagnosis catches
  • Problem 1 (wrong planning mode): verify_account and get_dispute_reason never depend on each other's results — this is a fixed-shape task (Section 4 of the lesson) being run emergently for no reason, costing extra reasoning calls with zero adaptivity gained, the same pattern as the lesson's own Section 9 comparison.
  • Problem 2 (wrong-plan failure): route_to_team's destination was locked in before get_dispute_reason's real result was known — the ticket needed the refunds team, not billing, and every tool call still succeeded. This is exactly Section 11's failure class: the plan itself was wrong, not any individual step.
  • A shallow AI answer often catches only Problem 2 (the more visible, more consequential-sounding one) and misses that Problem 1 is a separate, independent issue — fixing the misrouting alone (e.g., by making step 3 emergent) doesn't address the wasted reasoning calls on steps 1-2, and vice versa.

Compare the AI's answer against your own Step 1 notes and the two-problem breakdown above. Did it catch both? Did it propose the right fix for each — switching steps 1-2 to a fixed plan, and making step 3's team choice depend on step 2's actual result — or did it conflate the two into one vague "improve the planning" suggestion?
