Now Pair With an AI

A ninth scenario, not in the eight above: "ConciergeBot" at a fictional apartment complex answers maintenance questions using one tool, get_ticket_status(ticket_id). A resident asks "what's the status of my maintenance request," and ConciergeBot calls get_ticket_status({"ticket_id": "my request"}) — a string that was never a real ticket ID, just the resident's own phrasing carried through. The lookup returns {"error": "not found"}, and ConciergeBot tells the resident "your request is being processed," inventing a status it never actually retrieved.

Step 1: Diagnose it yourself, first

Before reading further: is this a wrong-tool-choice failure, a wrong-argument failure, or a hallucinated-result failure — and is it possibly more than one at once? Write your own answer before moving on.

Step 2: Ask an AI assistant

Thought process: this scenario has two layered problems (a bad argument AND a fabricated final answer), so the prompt needs to ask the AI to identify both, not just one — a shallow prompt like "what's wrong with this agent" invites a single, incomplete answer.

Write the prompt you'd give an AI assistant:

Paste your prompt into your assistant of choice and compare its answer against the critique below.
Show a strong version of this prompt 
Here's one exchange from my agent ConciergeBot, which has one tool, get_ticket_status(ticket_id): [paste the scenario above]. Identify EVERY distinct failure in this single exchange, not just the first one you notice -- there may be more than one -- and name each using this chapter's vocabulary (wrong tool choice, wrong argument, or hallucinated result despite/without a real observation). Propose a concrete fix for each failure you find.

Step 3: Critique what came back

What a complete diagnosis catches
  • Failure 1 (wrong argument): "my request" was never a real ticket ID — the tool needed the resident's actual ticket number, and nothing in the conversation extracted or asked for one before calling the tool.
  • Failure 2 (hallucinated result): the tool came back with an explicit {"error": "not found"} — a real, honest observation — and ConciergeBot's final answer ignored it entirely, inventing "being processed" instead of relaying the actual error or asking the resident for a valid ticket ID.
  • A shallow AI answer often catches only Failure 2 (the more visible, more dramatic-sounding one) and misses that Failure 1 is the reason Failure 2's observation was an error in the first place — they're connected, not independent.

Compare the AI's answer against your own Step 1 notes and the two-failure breakdown above. Did it catch both? Did it connect them, or treat them as unrelated?
