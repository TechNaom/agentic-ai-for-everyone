Now Pair With an AI

You already worked the exercises yourself on Summit Gear Co-op's GearBot. Now a third scenario, Fernbrook Ski Patrol (a fictional resort operations team) and its lift-status agent LiftBot, gets diagnosed a second way: by prompting an AI assistant, and reviewing what it hands back with the same skepticism you'd bring to a coworker's write-up before it ships to the lift operations lead.

Step 1: Diagnose it yourself, first

LiftBot has two tools, get_lift_status(lift_name) and get_wait_time(lift_name). Fernbrook's ops lead reports three real, observed problems:

  1. LiftBot answered "Is the Summit Express lift running?" by calling get_lift_status({"lift_name": "Summit Express lift"}) against data keyed as "summit express", and got back {"error": "not found"} — then told the guest "I don't have current lift data" instead of retrying with a cleaned-up name.
  2. During a stress test, a developer disconnected the tool's backing database mid-call. LiftBot's loop had no max_iterations, and it re-requested get_lift_status forever, since a connection error isn't a "final answer with no tool_calls."
  3. LiftBot's system prompt says "Answer skiing questions helpfully," with no mention of the two tools' names or what they check — and it frequently answers "is the gondola open" by guessing from general skiing knowledge instead of calling get_lift_status at all.

Before reading further, write your own diagnosis: for each of the three problems, name the concept it's really about (wrong-argument failure, missing guard, or a tool-description/reasoning gap) and propose one concrete fix. Use a notes app or scratch file — there's no grading here, just your own reasoning to compare against Step 3.

Step 2: Ask an AI assistant the same question

Thought process: the prompt needs to hand over all three problems with their real, specific evidence — not a vague "why is my agent broken" — or the AI has nothing concrete to diagnose and will produce generic advice instead of a checkable answer.

Write the prompt you'd give an AI assistant:

Paste your prompt into Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot, or whatever assistant you use, and compare what it builds against this page's own critique in Step 3.
Show a strong version of this prompt 
I have an agent called LiftBot with two tools, get_lift_status(lift_name) and get_wait_time(lift_name). Here are three real, observed problems: [paste the three numbered problems above verbatim]. For each problem, name which agent-loop concept it's really about (wrong-argument failure, missing iteration guard, or a tool-description/reasoning gap) and propose one concrete, specific code-level fix for each -- not a general "be more careful" suggestion.

Step 3: Critique what came back

A plausible-but-flawed AI answer to this exact prompt tends to make three specific mistakes worth watching for:

Common AI diagnosis mistakes on this scenario
  • Mislabeling Problem 1 as a "wrong tool choice." LiftBot picked the correct tool (get_lift_status) — the argument value just didn't match the data's exact key. A generic-sounding answer often says "the model should have picked a different tool," which is wrong; the real fix (a normalize step in the tool, per Section 9 of the lesson) never gets proposed if the diagnosis itself is wrong.
  • Proposing "add a retry" for Problem 2 without also proposing a hard cap. A retry on a connection error is reasonable, but without max_iterations as well, a persistent connection failure still loops forever — a subtler version of exactly the same guard-missing bug.
  • Treating Problem 3 as a prompting issue only. A vague system prompt is part of it, but the real, specific fix from this chapter is a better tool description (Section 4) — the field the model actually uses to decide whether a tool applies — not just a longer system message.

Compare the AI's actual answer against your own Step 1 notes and the three watch-outs above. Which one(s) did it get right? Which, if any, did it get wrong the way described here? That comparison — not the AI's fluency — is the real test.
